# Hopeling School - the full design

The ambition, in the user's future words: "I downloaded Hopeling
because I cared about nature. I kept it because it taught me how
to understand it."

The philosophy in one law, added to the constitution:

    NOTHING IS LEARNED UNTIL IT IS SEEN.
    Every lesson ends outside. Knowledge -> observation -> action
    is not a feature; it is the grading system.

What already exists and becomes School infrastructure: the
almanac engine (time-true content), the Living Sky (real sky,
real moon, computed offline), Paths (learning as walking, notes
not points), Mysteries (weekday clue rhythm), the Field Guide
(evidence, not scores), the Stillness (patience as mechanic),
the Hub (place awareness, iNaturalist reads), the fable voice,
and the constitution (deterministic reveals, no points, no
chatbot for children, every child-facing word human-reviewed).
School is not a new wing bolted on; it is these organs growing
a nervous system.

## PART I - THE FIFTEEN

### 1. The Errand (interactive field classes) - SIGNATURE
Every lesson ends with a card that cannot be completed on the
phone: "Now go find a leaf the caterpillars have been eating.
Look for the lace." The errand is generated from season + place
+ hour by the almanac (a February errand differs from a July
one), and completing it is self-reported the Hopeling way - you
write the field note; nobody checks; the note is the point.
Errands feed the Field Guide as evidence pages. What makes it
special: the homework is outside, and the app trusts you. This
single mechanic converts every piece of content we ever write
into a reason to step out the door. Build cost: small - a
per-lesson errand line plus almanac filtering - which is why it
is first.

### 2. The Lab ("What Would Happen If?") - SIGNATURE
Hand-built, deterministic ecosystem simulations - a few sliders,
living consequences. Meadow web: bees, wildflowers, rabbits,
foxes. Remove the bees, watch three seasons unfold. The Wolves
scenario replays Yellowstone 1995 as an interactive story - the
user makes the call the rangers made, then sees the rivers
change course, and THEN learns it really happened (citation on
screen). Ocean warming: one degree, coral, fish, fishermen.
The rules: every model is small, honest about being a model,
and deterministic - same inputs, same outcome, because science
is reproducible and we do not run slot machines. Kids get the
same engines reskinned as "pull one thread" toys with the fable
voice narrating consequences gently (no doom framing - the last
act of every kid scenario is repair). What makes it special:
nobody in the nature-app world ships real interactive ecology;
it is the difference between reading about food webs and
FEELING one tug back.

### 3. The Listening Post (ear training) - SIGNATURE
Real recordings via the xeno-canto API (keyless, like all our
data partners) curated into tiny ear-courses: the five voices
of YOUR area this season, chosen by Hub area + almanac. Learn
robin vs wren in ninety seconds a day; the quiz is hearing, not
reading. Graduation errand: the Dawn Sit - step outside at
first light, count the voices you now know, write them down.
Adults go deep: call versus song, mimicry, the dawn chorus
order (who sings first and why). Kids version: "who is
singing?" - three big buttons, fable voice, pure play, no
scores. What makes it special: ear-training is the fastest
transformation we can sell - in one week the morning racket
becomes a room full of neighbors with names. The world sounds
different forever. That is the brand promise made literal.

### 4. Detective School (the pedagogy of clues)
The weekly Mystery grows a faculty. Skills are taught AS cases:
the tracks course is a whodunit where each clue teaches a real
skill (gait patterns, claw marks, direction of travel). The
crown jewel is the INTERACTIVE DICHOTOMOUS KEY - the real
naturalist's tool, turned into UI: "Leaf edges smooth or
toothed?" Tap, tap, tap - you have keyed out an oak, and you
learned WHY it is an oak, which no image-recognition magic
teaches. Keys for: common trees, tracks, feathers (with the
one-line legal note), garden insects. Field-usable offline.
What makes it special: identification apps give answers;
Hopeling teaches the questions. One is a vending machine, the
other is an education.

### 5. The Crafts (field skills as apprenticeships)
Cloud reading (wired to Open-Meteo, keyless: "today over you:
cumulus building - here is what that means for the evening"),
star finding (computed offline like our moon - tonight's actual
sky, drawn), tide reading (NOAA keyless for North America - the
flagship market gets tides at launch), tracking, ethical
wildlife photography, safe observation distances. Each craft
progresses apprentice -> journeyman -> keeper, and progress is
artifacts: your cloud diary, your star log, your first tide
table read correctly. What makes it special: these are the
skills people wish someone had taught them; each one converts
a daily glance (sky, night, sea) into reading a language.

### 6. One Wonder (micro-courses)
The Daily Wonder grows a shelf: two-minute illustrated lessons,
one incredible thing each - why the moon follows your car, how
a swift sleeps flying, what mushrooms actually are. Every one
ends with tonight's errand. Deterministic daily pick from the
deck, month-tuned like the almanac. The gateway drug of the
whole School: zero commitment, full wonder.

### 7. The Long Paths + the Deep Layer (expert mode)
Paths grow graduate chapters that assume the walking already
done - and the Atlas gains a register toggle: every species
page can be read as the kid line, the naturalist line, or the
scholar layer (annotated further-reading doors, real papers,
open questions scientists still argue about). Depth is unlocked
by field notes walked, not quizzes passed - evidence of looking,
not memory of reading. What makes it special: it answers the
co-founder's oldest question - one app for everyone - with
architecture instead of compromise: one valley, three depths of
seeing, chosen page by page.

### 8. The Expedition (family school)
Weekend co-missions with roles: the child is the Eyes, the
adult is the Scribe. The parent unlocks it from the adult side;
the kid receives it in kids mode as a pure adventure (the
constitution holds: no links, no locations, no pressure in the
kid flow - the parent carries the map). It ends in one shared
field note, signed by both, kept in both guides. Pond dipping,
moon walking, the worm census after rain. What makes it
special: almost no product designs for the parent-and-child AS
A UNIT; we make the family the classroom and the memory the
diploma.

### 9. The Citizen's Path (real science, staged honestly)
A path that ends outside the app: learn to observe -> learn to
document (the photo standards that make an observation
scientifically usable) -> graduate to your first real
iNaturalist contribution (adult door, external by design). We
already READ iNaturalist into the Hub; this closes the loop -
Hopeling users start feeding the very dataset that makes their
own Hub alive. What makes it special: the graduation is not a
certificate, it is membership in actual science.

### 10. Seasonal School (the sky writes the syllabus)
Courses schedule themselves by the almanac: the migration
course opens when migration begins over you; fungi school
arrives with autumn rain; tide-pool school with the summer low
tides; the star course deepens as nights lengthen. Nothing is
ever "locked" - everything can be browsed - but the front hall
always leads with what is ALIVE right now. What makes it
special: every other platform's curriculum is frozen; ours is
weather. And it is nearly free to build - the almanac engine
already knows the dates.

### 11. Local School (your valley, 101)
With the Hub area set: your watershed and where your rain goes,
your keystone species, your invasives and why they matter, your
region's conservation story, editorial packs per region
(co-founder's regions first, honest about coverage like
everything we ship). What makes it special: "nature" stops
being a documentary category and becomes YOUR street's cast
list.

### 12. The Grove of Knowing (the anti-progress-bar) - SIGNATURE
No percentages anywhere. Your understanding is drawn as a
living web - each concept a node drawn like a species or place,
each RELATIONSHIP you learn a mycorrhizal thread between them.
Learn bees, learn wildflowers: two nodes. Learn what connects
them: the thread lights. Over months the web becomes dense,
personal, beautiful - and visibly incomplete at the edges,
because ecology is. Tap any thread to revisit the lesson that
built it. What makes it special: it teaches the deepest lesson
in ecology - that knowledge is connection - through its own
form, and it is a screenshot people will share unprompted.

### 13. The Rites (real-world graduation) - SIGNATURE
Long paths do not end in certificates; they end OUTSIDE, in a
rite: the Dawn Sit (birds), the Pollinator Patch (a square
meter you plant and tend), the Tide Pool Hour, the Backyard
Biodiversity Survey (one hour, count everything alive). The
rite produces a Charter - an illustrated field-guide page in
the Dawnlight style recording what you did, where, and what you
found - an artifact, never a badge. What makes it special: the
diploma is a changed patch of the actual world.

### 14. The Keeper's Desk (asking, the honest way)
Adult-only. Not a chatbot persona: a question box that answers
FROM the curated corpus - lessons, Atlas pages, almanac - with
citations into the app, in the fable register. When the corpus
cannot answer, it says so plainly, and the question joins the
editorial queue: real user questions become next fortnight's
micro-lessons (credited: "asked by a keeper in Ohio"). The
school learns what to teach from what its students wonder. For
children: never a chatbot (constitution); instead the Wonder
Jar - a child's question, typed by the parent, may return days
later as a human-written, fable-voiced answer. Slower than AI.
Better than AI, for a seven-year-old. What makes it special:
we turn the AI question from a risk into an editorial flywheel,
and the delay itself teaches that good answers are worth
waiting for.

### 15. The Schoolhouse (the UI is a place, not a list)
Entering School is entering a building on the hillside - drawn,
warm, seasonal: the Notice Board ("this fortnight over your
valley"), doors to the Lab, the Listening Post, the Field Desk
(errands and crafts), the Library (paths, one-wonder shelf),
the Map Wall (Grove of Knowing), the Rites board by the door.
The hall changes with the almanac - autumn light in autumn.
Kids NEVER see "School": their versions of these engines live
inside their existing rooms as adventures, games, and stories,
with no curriculum language at all - discovery through play is
not a mode, it is their whole world.

## PART II - THE THREE UNASKED

### S1. The Almanac Bet (phenology as a personal wager)
Each season, you predict your valley: the date of the first
swallow, the first frost, the first frog chorus. The app
records your bets; nature grades them; your accuracy grows
year over year until YOU are the local almanac - and your
decade of dates becomes something science calls phenology and
grandparents call memory. Family and classroom versions turn
it into the gentlest competition in software: everyone against
the sky. And quietly, personally, it makes climate change
visible without a single lecture: your own dates, drifting.
Could become the most Hopeling feature in Hopeling.

### S2. The Sit Spot (the oldest curriculum on Earth)
One place, chosen once. Ten minutes, once a week, same spot,
every season. The app is deliberately almost absent: it asks
one question before (what do you expect?) and one after (what
happened that you did not expect?), and keeps the sit spot
journal as its own Field Guide chapter. Over a year, the same
three meters teach seasons, succession, weather, patience, and
the difference between looking and seeing - the entire
curriculum, taught by a bush. The Stillness mechanic was the
rehearsal; this is the real thing. Kids version: "my secret
spot," with a guardian along.

### S3. The Teachback (you graduate by teaching)
The final chapter of every long path is not a test: you teach
it. Record or write your own two-minute lesson aimed at one
real person - your kid, your friend, your class. The protege
effect is one of the best-replicated results in learning
science: nothing organizes knowledge like preparing to give it
away. Kids teach back by drawing and telling (fable structure:
who, where, one wonder). And the flywheel: submitted teachbacks
that pass human review can join the One Wonder shelf, credited
- "taught by Maya, 9, Ohio" - making Hopeling's community
visible the only way our constitution allows: not feeds, not
comments - teaching.

## PART III - WHAT I WOULD ACTUALLY BUILD (in order)

1. THE ERRAND on existing Paths + Wonders (small code, changes
   everything; the law becomes real).
2. THE SCHOOLHOUSE HALL v1 - re-house Learn+Paths+Mysteries
   behind the drawn hall with the Notice Board (almanac-driven).
   No new content needed; the world-feeling arrives.
3. THE LISTENING POST v1 - xeno-canto, ten local birds, the
   Dawn Sit rite. First signature live.
4. THE LAB v1 - three sims: Meadow Web, The Wolves, No More
   Bees. Deterministic, cited, kid reskins after adult ships.
5. THE GROVE OF KNOWING v1 - nodes and threads for existing
   paths + new content as it lands.
6. THE RITES v1 - Dawn Sit + Backyard Survey, with Charters.
7. THE ALMANAC BET v1 - three bets per season, solo + family.
8. Then: Detective keys, Crafts (clouds first - Open-Meteo),
   Local School packs (co-founder's regions), Citizen's Path,
   the Keeper's Desk, Sit Spot, Teachback, Deep Layer.

SHIPPED SO FAR: the Errand (the first law, made real), the Lab
(twelve experiments, three living worlds, the threshold hunt,
the Two Benches), the Listening Post (five voices, credited
recordists, self-hosted audio), the Little Meadow (the kids'
first Lab, repair-always), and now THE CLASSROOM BENCH.

THE CLASSROOM BENCH is the first door in this building built
for the grown-up rather than the learner: any experiment can be
run with a whole room, and the room predicts together before
anything moves. It carries twelve hand-written lesson plans,
each including the thing lesson plans never admit - what the
room usually says first, and why that answer is reasonable. The
class splits into groups, every group's lever runs at once on
one shared clock, and the disagreement between the room and the
model is named as the best possible outcome rather than the
failed one. Nothing is scored, nobody is named, no hand is ever
stored. See LAB.md section 11.

The content bottleneck stays honest: the engines are mine to
build; the words stay human - the co-founder's courses, the
review gates, the fable voice. The School can only grow as
fast as its keepers write, and that is a feature: it is why
every word will deserve a child's trust.

## PART IV - what School must never become

No points, no streak guilt, no leaderboards, no locked content
as pressure (locked-by-season is rhythm, not paywall), no
AI-generated child-facing words, no fake "live" data, no quiz
anxiety (the only exam is outside, ungraded, and beautiful),
and no course-completion percentages - ever. The Grove of
Knowing grows; it does not fill.
