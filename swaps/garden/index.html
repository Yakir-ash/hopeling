The hedgehog eats the slug that ate the pellet. Poison climbs the food chain one meal at a time.

What to avoid, and how it hides on a label

metaldehyde slug pellets
ON A LABEL: METALDEHYDE, META PELLETS

The slug eats the pellet, the hedgehog eats the slug. Banned for outdoor use in the UK in 2022 for exactly that reason: birds, toads and hedgehogs were dying second-hand.

neonicotinoid insecticides
ON A LABEL: IMIDACLOPRID, THIAMETHOXAM, CLOTHIANIDIN, ACETAMIPRID

A systemic insecticide is in the whole plant, pollen included. The EU banned outdoor use of the main three in 2018 for the bees; the same molecules are still sold for gardens in much of the world, imidacloprid most of all.

What to prefer

nothing, and a slightly untidy garden

A hedgehog and a thrush are slug control. They need somewhere to sleep and something to eat, which is the untidy corner.

ferric phosphate pellets, if you must

Slugs stop feeding; the predators that eat the slugs are not poisoned in turn. Less effective, and that is the trade.

hand-picking at dusk, with a torch

Ten minutes, no chemistry, and you meet the night shift of your own garden.

The pick, honestly

No sprays, no pellets, one untidy corner left for the helpers

This is the one card where the best product is no product. If you buy anything, buy the ferric phosphate kind and read the active ingredient, not the front.

No brand is named here yet. When one is, it will carry the date a person checked it, and it will never move the pick: there is no ranking to move.

If that is too much today

Spray only at dusk, and never on anything in flower

If you cannot go without, the bees are home by dusk and the flowers are where they feed. It is a smaller change that removes most of the harm.

The chain

pellet → slug → hedgehog → fewer helpers → more slugs

Where this comes from

  • Metaldehyde slug pellets banned in the UK - Hedgehog Street (PTES and BHPS). outdoor sale and use banned from 1 April 2022 following advice on risks to birds, toads and hedgehogs; ferric phosphate named as the alternative.
  • Metaldehyde slug pellets to be banned from spring 2022 - Farmers Weekly, reporting Defra. the decision was taken to better protect wildlife; harm through direct ingestion or contaminated molluscs.
  • An overview of agricultural neonicotinoid regulation in the EU, Canada, and the United States - PMC (peer-reviewed review). full outdoor ban on imidacloprid, clothianidin and thiamethoxam in the EU in 2018; thiacloprid withdrawn 2020.
  • EPA Actions to Protect Pollinators - US EPA. label language advising homeowners not to use neonicotinoid products; residential turf spray uses of imidacloprid cancelled.
