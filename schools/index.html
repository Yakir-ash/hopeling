What one lesson looks like

The teacher puts one sentence on the board. The whole room predicts, out loud, counted and never graded. Each group pulls a different lever, and every world runs at once on one clock. Then the room argues about the gap between what it expected and what the model did.

  6. CloseThe repair, run in front of them. The way back is slower than the way down.

What the room usually says first

The room answers "the flowers" almost immediately, and that answer is right and it is the easy half. Hold the question open until somebody says a word that is not a plant. The lesson lives in the gap between those two answers.

Three prompts

  1. Which line moved first, and which line moved most? They are not the same line, and that is the whole idea.
  2. No fox ever met a bee. Trace the path out loud, animal by animal, until you get from a bee to a fox.
  3. Name one thing in this building that would notice if the bees went. Now name the thing that would notice second.

The sentence to leave standing

Nothing in the meadow starves on the first day. That is exactly what makes it hard to see in time.

The six weeks

One 45-minute lesson a week. Every one of them ends outside.

LessonWith HopelingOutside
1Meet your school ecosystemThe real sky at this hour; the neighbours by the hourTen minutes of sitting still, and a list

3Investigate biodiversityThe Listening Post; first signs of the seasonA count by eye and by ear, on a walk you can repeat

5Design and make one improvementThe experiment's own repairPlant, build, switch or shade. Something physical begins

Assessment stays in the teacher's hands and off the child's screen: the show of hands before the run, and each student's own explanation after it.

What is different about it

Nothing is learned until it is seen
The School's first law. Every lesson ends with something to notice outside, and the daily Errand cannot be done on a phone.
Honest models
Every experiment runs three ways: cautious, best estimate, severe. Every thread in the web can be opened to see why it is there, and every model says what it leaves out.
Nothing is scored
No points, no streaks, no leaderboards, no timers. A prediction is how a scientist says hello to a question.
Repair, always
Each experiment that breaks something offers the way back, and shows that recovery is real and slower than never losing.
A teacher runs the room
The Bench runs the lesson so the teacher does not have to. It does not replace her.

Privacy and safety

In the classroom
The Classroom Bench needs no student accounts and collects no student information. The teacher projects, the class answers out loud, and the show of hands is never saved.
Hopeling Kids
Behind a parent's gate. No ads, no chat, no strangers, no location, no camera, no microphone, nothing to buy.
Agreements
We have not yet signed a student data privacy agreement with any district. We will with our first pilot partners, and holding no student data is the position we intend to keep.
A pilot, concretely

Two or three classrooms, six weeks, free.

We bring the unit, the lesson plans and ourselves on the phone. At the end you get a one-page report of what was counted, what was planted and what your students said, to use however you like.

  • We ask for one teacher who will tell us honestly what worked and what did not
  • We ask to share the report, without any student names, with other schools
  • Aquariums, museums, watershed groups and outdoor programs: the unit can be the six weeks after your field trip
